Microwave-frequency acoustic waves in solids provide a compact and versatile platform for classical signal processing, quantum phononics, and hybrid integrated systems. A practical phononic integrated circuit (PnIC) platform, however, needs to combine low-loss waveguides, high-quality-factor resonators, efficient electromechanical transducers, and scalable circuit components on the same chip.

We developed a low-loss gigahertz-frequency PnIC platform based on patterned thin-film silicon nitride (SiN) on a lithium niobate (LN) substrate {% cite ji2026lowloss %}. This hybrid SiN-LN platform supports key phononic circuit building blocks, including low-loss waveguides, directional couplers, and high-Q ring resonators, while retaining the strong piezoelectric response of LN for efficient electrical access.

As a system-level demonstration, we realized a 1-GHz phononic oscillator based on a ring resonator, achieving a phase noise of -159.0 dBc/Hz at a 100-kHz offset frequency. These low-loss PnICs provide a route toward scalable microwave acoustics and integrated hybrid systems that connect phonons with photons, superconducting qubits, and solid-state defects.
